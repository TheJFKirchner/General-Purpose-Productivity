Themes

Show

FR-01 title

Large headline, kicker above, one line below, no visual; for opening with the answer or the subject, or telling the audience who is presenting and when.

FR-02 section-divider

Section name large, one line saying what the section will show; for marking a change of topic in a longer deck, or giving the audience a breath before a new line of argument.

FR-03 agenda-rows

Numbered rows: part name and one line each; for showing the route through a longer deck, or matching what is said to the sections in the bottom bar.

FR-04 goal-three-cards

Three cards side by side, the third tinted; for reminding the audience of the original goal, or laying out problem, action and measure of success in one view.

FR-05 statement-quote

One sentence or quote in large type, source line under it; for making the audience hear one message with nothing competing, or letting a customer or stakeholder say it in their own words.

EX-01 steps-flow

Three to five steps on a line, each with a title and one line; for showing a method or process in order, or showing a timeline or plan with the current point marked.

EX-02 data-two-cards

Two cards: what was used (rows) and how it was checked (bullets); for showing what the analysis rests on and why it can be trusted, or answering scope and data-quality questions before they are asked.

LV-01 stats-three

Three large figures in a row, each with a label and a comparison note, one sentence under them, no chart; for landing two or three results at once, or showing results against target or prior without a chart.

LV-02 hero-number-line

One very large figure with its meaning in plain words, a line chart beside it; for making one number the whole message, or showing the figure and the path that led to it.

LV-03 hero-number-quote

One very large figure beside a one-sentence statement; for closing on the one thing to remember, or pairing a figure with the sentence that explains it.

LV-04 stats-over-bars

Three figure cards across the top, a bar chart and two bullets under; for stating a position with its two inputs and the gap between them, or showing the headline figures and how one of them has moved.

LV-05 bars-highlight-commentary

Vertical bars with one highlighted, commentary bullets and a so-what line on the right; for showing which category stands out, or comparing a handful of groups on one measure and saying why one differs.

LV-06 bars-ranked-commentary

Sorted horizontal bars with the leader highlighted, fuller commentary on the right; for ranking items when the labels are long, or showing which item drives a result.

CH-01 line-fit-commentary

Actual line with a dashed fitted line, commentary and so-what on the right; for showing a steady rate of change, or showing how closely actuals follow a fitted or expected path.

CH-02 line-event-callout

Callout with one figure and three bullets on the left; two lines with an event marked on the right; for showing the effect of a change against a group that did not change, or tying a movement to the moment that caused it.

CH-03 dots-two-fits

Commentary on the left; actual points with a longer and a shorter fitted line on the right; for asking whether a rate of change is speeding up or slowing, or comparing a long-run view with a recent view of the same measure.

CH-04 bars-outlier-callout

One callout sentence over full-width bars with one period highlighted; for calling out an unusual period and how it was treated, or a lean slide for a senior audience: one sentence, one picture.

CH-05 bars-before-after

Callout with the size of the change and two bullets; bars by period with the later periods highlighted; for showing a level shift after a change, or stating the size of an effect next to the evidence for it.

CH-06 two-charts-bullets

Two small line charts side by side, one muted and one highlighted, three bullets in a row under them; for splitting a result into two measures to show which one is moving, or showing that one thing is stable while another is not.

CH-07 change-cards-bars

Three stacked cards, each a change with its size and direction, beside a bar chart of the total at each review; for saying what is different since the audience last saw this, or listing a few changes with their sizes alongside the overall movement.

CP-01 paired-bars-commentary

Paired bars by category (two series), commentary and so-what on the right; for comparing actual with expected across a few groups, or showing where two measures agree and where they part.

CP-02 bars-target-tick

Horizontal bars with a tick marking the target for each, the miss highlighted; commentary on the right; for showing performance against a target or plan for each item, or picking out the one item that missed.

CP-03 two-lines-fuller-commentary

Fuller commentary on the left, two lines over time on the right; for comparing our experience with a benchmark or peer, or a comparison meant to be read without a presenter.

CP-04 two-lines-choice-cards

Two lines over time, then one card per alternative with the chosen one tinted; for showing two approaches that give different answers and which was chosen, or explaining a judgement call between two views.

CP-05 dumbbell-callout

Full-width dumbbell chart (two points joined per category), one sentence under it; for showing movement between two points for several items at once, or showing which items moved most and in which direction.

CP-06 two-numbers-difference

Two large figures in cards with the difference stated between them, no chart; for a before and after, or an old and new, in one glance, or when the difference is the message and a chart would add nothing.

CP-07 table-key-row-commentary

Small table with one highlighted row, commentary and so-what beside it; for showing plan, actual and difference where exact figures will be read, or any small table where one row carries the point.

CP-08 option-cards-recommendation

Two cards comparing options on the same rows, then the recommendation, no chart; for putting two options side by side on cost, benefit and risk, or leading the audience to a recommendation they can see the basis for.

BD-01 waterfall-callout

Full-width vertical waterfall from one figure to another, one-sentence callout under it; for bridging from a starting figure to an ending figure by cause, or showing which driver accounts for most of a change.

BD-02 waterfall-horizontal-commentary

Horizontal waterfall on the left, fuller commentary on the right; for building up a result from its components when labels are long, or a bridge that needs explaining step by step, readable without a presenter.

BD-03 number-small-waterfall

Callout with one large figure and two bullets on the left, a three-bar waterfall on the right; for stating the effect of one decision and showing where it lands, or a simple from, change, to story.

BD-04 rows-plus-bars

Three numbered rows on the left, the same three effects as bars on the right; for splitting a change into a few named effects, or giving each effect a sentence and showing their relative size.

BD-05 stacked-mix-shift

Two 100% stacked bars, bullets and a callout under them; for showing how the make-up of a total shifted between two points, or showing that a result changed because the mix changed.

BD-06 stats-over-stacked-bars

Three figures across the top, stacked horizontal bars by category, commentary beside; for splitting a total into two factors across several categories, or showing the headline figures and which category is behind them.

BD-07 hero-number-donut

One very large figure in a callout with a two-part doughnut, no commentary; for showing that one part explains most of the whole, or a lean slide where a single share is the message.

BD-08 table-bars-sensitivity

Small table and two bullets on the left, bars for each scenario on the right; for showing what the result becomes under different assumptions, or giving exact figures and their shape together.

UN-01 range-bar-commentary

One low-to-high range bar with the central and held figures marked, commentary under it; for showing where a chosen figure sits within a reasonable range, or making the size of the uncertainty visible.

UN-02 scenario-bars-caveats

Scenario bars on the left, four caveats and a so-what line on the right; for telling the audience what could change the answer before they decide, or sizing a few scenarios against the central view.

DE-01 summary-stats-ask

Answer as the headline, three large figures, the ask in a strip along the bottom; for an executive summary on one slide, or giving the answer, its support and the ask together.

DE-02 numbered-findings

Numbered rows, each a finding with its figure on the right; for listing the takeaways with their sizes, or listing risks or issues ranked by size.

DE-03 audience-rows

One row per audience group: who, what it means for them, the one figure; for translating a result for each group in the room, or showing each team what changes for them.

DE-04 scorecard-status

Table with a status dot per row, one row highlighted, one line under it; for giving the status of several items at a glance, or showing which item needs attention.

DE-05 decision-two-paths

The decision in a callout, then one card for what happens if approved and one for if it waits; for asking for a decision and showing the consequence of each answer, or making the cost of delay explicit.

DE-06 ask-and-actions

The decision needed in a callout on the left, rows of action, timing and owner on the right; for closing with the ask and who does what by when, or any decision followed by a short action list.

DE-07 ask-and-timeline

A starting point in a callout over a five-step timeline with dates and owners; for showing the sequence of work that follows a decision, or a plan where order and dates matter more than detail.

BK-01 answer-with-bars

The answer as the headline; the question, a short answer and horizontal bars below; for answering a question the audience is likely to ask, or holding supporting evidence without lengthening the main story.

BK-02 heatmap-callout

One callout sentence over a small heatmap grid with one diagonal highlighted; for showing a pattern across two dimensions, such as period by age, or pointing at one part of a grid of figures.

CT-01 bar-highlight

Vertical bars, one highlighted in blue, the rest grey, values on top; for showing which category stands out, or comparing a handful of groups on one measure.

CT-02 bars-ranked

Horizontal bars sorted largest first, the leader in blue, values at the bar ends; for ranking items when the labels are long, or showing which item drives a result.

CT-03 line-event

Two lines over time, the one that matters in blue, both ends labelled, an event marked with a dashed line; for showing the effect of a change against a group that did not change, or tying a movement to the moment that caused it.

CT-04 line-fit

Actual line in blue with a dashed grey fitted line, both ends labelled; for showing a steady rate of change, or showing how closely actuals follow a fitted or expected path.

CT-05 paired-bars

Two bars per category (expected in light grey, actual darker), the category that matters in blue, legend at top left; for comparing actual with expected across a few groups, or showing where two measures agree and where they part.

CT-06 waterfall

Vertical bridge from a starting total to an ending total; totals dark grey, the step that matters blue, other steps light grey; for bridging from a starting figure to an ending figure by cause, or showing which driver accounts for most of a change.

CT-07 target-tick-bars

Horizontal bars with a dark tick marking each item's target, the miss in blue with its value inside the bar; for showing performance against a target or plan for each item, or picking out the one item that missed.

CT-08 dumbbell

Two dots per item joined by a short bar, the earlier point light grey, the later dark, the item that matters in blue; for showing movement between two points for several items at once, or showing which items moved most and in which direction.

CT-09 stacked-share

Two 100% stacked horizontal bars, the part that changed in blue with its label inside, the other parts grey; for showing how the make-up of a total shifted between two points, or showing that a result changed because the mix changed.

CT-10 heatmap-grid

A small grid of figures on light grey cells, the cells that matter in blue with white text; for showing a pattern across two dimensions, such as period by age, or pointing at one part of a grid of figures.

CT-11 range-bar

One low-to-high grey bar with the central figure as a dashed line and the chosen figure as a solid blue line; for showing where a chosen figure sits within a reasonable range, or making the size of the uncertainty visible.

TH-01 light

The default look: white page, navy headings, one blue highlight against grey context, sharp corners.

TH-02 dark

Example dark theme: light text on a deep slate page, for screens in a dim room.

TH-03 print-grey

Example greyscale theme for print: black and greys only, serif headings, no colour needed.
