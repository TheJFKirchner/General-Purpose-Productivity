# Analytic spaces (draft, parked)

Status: draft (parked by the user; nothing approved, nothing built)
Date: 2026-11-17

An early scoping conversation about "analytic spaces": a way to start a new study on the basis of a known methodology without re-reading a whole wiki to find and check the pages that apply. The user parked it part-way through because it was turning into something specific to one kind of analytics work and overlaps with studies, which may be better served by workflows. It may be picked up again if spaces come naturally out of the workflows design.

## The problem being solved

To do a new study, a quick analysis or a test, a person has to read through their wiki, pull the pages that apply and check they are right. If the study is on the basis of a known methodology (the user's example: a large recurring report with many sub-studies done out of it), they should be able to go to a space dedicated to that methodology and run, or ask for, the analysis there. A second, separate problem: a main project plus many sub-studies gets messy on disk.

## Where the conversation got to

- **The clash with personal wikis.** People will already have their own markdown knowledge wikis. A space that is itself a folder of pages would be a second home for the same knowledge, and the two would drift.
- **Agreed direction: a space is a layer, not a store.** A space links mainly to markdown pages that already exist for its methodology, plus a light page or two that link them together and give routing and guidance for the different kinds of report. It holds no copy of the knowledge. Known cost: it depends on the pages it points to, so it needs a check that they still exist.
- **A repo per space was rejected** by the user as too messy.

## Left open (asked, not answered)

1. Where a space lives. Recommended: a routing page inside the wiki it draws from, marked as a space, found by Launchpad in any connected folder the way skills and agents are. It is then shared exactly as far as its wiki is shared.
2. What going to a space does. Recommended: pick a space when starting a plan or chat; the planner reads the routing page first, reads only the pages it routes to for that kind of study, and the plan and report name the pages relied on.
3. Sub-studies. Recommended: the space keeps a simple list of the studies run from it (name, date, question, link to the report), with study folders kept outside the wiki.

Also unanswered from the first round: whether a space is built for agents first with people able to browse it, and whether sessions treat a space as read-only with changes suggested to its owner.
