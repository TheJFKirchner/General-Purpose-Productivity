The brain

A shared notebook the assistant reads before it answers.

Why does the assistant need a brain?

Because it starts every conversation knowing nothing about your work. So you explain the same things again: what a column means, which table to trust, why a number is defined that way. And when one person figures something out, it stays with them.

  • Explain a thing once, not every time.
  • What one person learns, the team gets.

So what is it, actually?

A folder of short note pages, one per topic, kept as plain files (markdown, if you know it). Some pages are yours alone. Others are shared with the team. Before the assistant answers, it reads the ones that matter.

Does it read the whole thing every time?

No. For a question about claims trend, the assistant above opens 11 of the 22 pages and leaves the rest closed. It picks pages by what they say about the question, and it opens only the part that applies.

  • Shorter conversations.
  • The same answer for everyone, because it comes from the same pages.

How does something get into the brain?

While you work, the assistant jots down facts worth keeping, one line each. Later, when you ask it to, it files each one onto the right personal page, or starts a new page if the topic has none. Sharing is your call: you pick which lines move to the team page.

How it works, step by step

  1. Something comes up

    The assistant notes it as one plain line.

  2. Intake files it

    It goes onto the right personal page.

  3. You share it

    Pick lines to move to the team page.

  4. Later sessions read it

    You or a colleague, on a similar task.

Four skills

A skill is a written guide the assistant follows.

  • brain-file files the facts noted so far. Only when you ask.
  • brain-ingest pulls facts out of documents you drop in a folder.
  • brain-promote moves lines you pick to the team page.
  • brain-cite lists the pages worth reading for your task.

Four helpers

A helper is a small assistant started for one job.

  • brain-filer decides where each fact goes and writes it.
  • brain-extractor finds the lasting facts in documents.
  • brain-promoter adds picked lines to the team page.
  • brain-context-analyst picks the few pages worth reading.

What goes in, and what stays out?

Things that stay true go in. Results, secrets and private details stay out.

In

  • Definitions and methods
  • How data and systems behave, and known traps
  • Decisions, and the reason for them

Out

  • A project's results They go in that project's findings.
  • Passwords, keys and other secrets
  • Personal details about people

The brain is only as good as what gets put in. Keep each fact short, say where it came from, and keep anything private out.
