Against benchmark

Our medical cost trend has run above the industry benchmark for three years

How to read this

  • Until 2022 the two lines stayed within 0.2 points of each other.
  • From 2023 our plans pull away: the gap is 0.9 points, then 1.3, then 1.5.
  • Outpatient surgery explains 1.0 of the 1.5 points, as more procedures moved to hospital-owned sites.
  • Each point of trend is about $9m of claims a year on this block.

Pricing 2026 at the industry trend would leave rates about 1.5 points short.

Medical cost trend: yearly change in the cost of care per member, large group plans, 2020 to 2025. Benchmark: yearly trend survey of 40 regional health plans. Our figures: claims warehouse.

Options

Raising the reinsurance retention to $5m is worth $2.5m a year on average

Option A: keep the $2m retention

Cost

$14.8m of reinsurance premium a year.

Benefit

No single claim costs us more than $2m.

Risk

The price rose 12% at each of the last two renewals.

Option B: raise the retention to $5m

Cost

$8.4m of premium, plus $3.9m of extra claims we expect to keep.

Benefit

$2.5m a year better than option A on average.

Risk

One year in ten, the result is $8m worse than under option A.

We recommend option B from the 1 January renewal.

The one-in-ten bad year costs $8m, which is 2% of our $400m of capital.

Retention: the part of each claim we pay before reinsurance starts. Premiums from renewal quotes. Claims kept and the one-in-ten year from 10,000 simulated years of large claims.
